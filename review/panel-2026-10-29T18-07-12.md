## 3. Cold‑state data strategy – show market numbers or mask them?

**Recommendation:** **Show the market numbers (SPY‑based beta) now, mask the cultural column until Qloo data arrives.**  

### Why this satisfies a Qloo CTO

1. **Transparency of what *is* delivered** – The desk already provides a genuine, verifiable market‑beta regression. Hiding it would give the impression that the entire product is non‑functional, which undercuts confidence in the engineering effort that *is* working.

2. **Risk of “partial data” perception** – Judges (and the CTO) understand that the cultural factor is the novel part. By displaying the market side, you demonstrate that the pipeline (data fetch → regression → UI) works end‑to‑end for the *known* data source. The missing cultural column is then clearly a **dependency issue**, not a product flaw.

3. **Clear “data‑pending” signal** – When the cultural column is hidden or replaced with a deterministic placeholder (“— — —”), the UI tells the user *exactly* what is missing. This is preferable to a half‑filled bar that looks like a loading glitch.

4. **Avoids “double‑masking”** – Masking both sides would make the desk look empty, forcing the demo to spend time explaining why nothing is shown. Showing the market side lets you spend the demo time on *why* the cultural beta matters, not on why you can’t show anything.

5. **Future‑proof** – Once the key arrives, you only need to un‑hide the cultural column; no redesign is required.

**Implementation tip:** Wrap the cultural‑beta column in a `<div class="cultural-col hidden-until-data">`. When the first successful Qloo response is cached, toggle the class to reveal the column. The hidden state should include a tooltip: “Cultural beta loading – Qloo API key pending”.

## 4. Is ΔR² + flag + limits card enough for a quant judge?

**Short answer:** **Almost enough, but add a single “t‑stat of the cultural beta”** (or its absolute value) as a quantitative confidence metric.

### What the extra number buys

* **Statistical significance at a glance** – The flag tells you when β < 0 & |t| ≥ 2, but judges often want to see the *strength* of that significance across the whole book, not just a binary flag. Displaying the **average |t|** (or median) of the cultural betas gives a sense of overall signal‑to‑noise.

* **Model robustness check** – ΔR² shows *incremental explanatory power*, the flag shows *directional risk*, and the t‑stat shows *precision*. Together they answer the three classic regression questions: **how much**, **which way**, **how certain**.

* **No extra UI clutter** – The t‑stat can be placed next to the cultural‑beta value inside the same cell, in a smaller, muted font (e.g., `β̂ = 0.12 ± 0.04  (t = 3.1)`). This is a one‑line addition that does not require a new component.

Thus, the quant judge gets a complete picture: incremental R², sign‑risk flag, and statistical confidence.
