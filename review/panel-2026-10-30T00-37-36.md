## Abstract reasoning (what is being measured, what would falsify it, what it's worth if negative)

**What is being measured.** The entry measures *exposure*: how much of a consumer stock's weekly return a taste-momentum factor (built from Qloo's cultural graph) explains over and above the S&P 500, and — the new and important part — over and above a **same-sector peer basket** that carries the identical taste exposure with zero Qloo input. The instrument is a two-regressor OLS (SPY + basket); the estimator is the cultural beta plus its t, BH q, and OOS ΔR²; the control is the peer-only arm.

**What would falsify it.** The headline claim ("Qloo adds a clean instrument for taste risk that the sector already shows you") is falsified by the ablation band going negative *out of sample* — peer-only forecast beats peer+taste. Note the asymmetry that the team itself documents: because peers carry the same taste, a **positive** OOS gain is only a *lower bound* on the instrument's value; a **negative** one is not a lower bound on absence — it is confounded by the overlap. So the control is strong evidence against, weak evidence for. The rotation placebo and the disjoint second window falsify the "factor is real and not an artifact" half; the primary window already shows p 0.531 / "does not replicate," so on the attached stub the whole factor is currently *unproven*.

**Does the instrument/estimator/control establish what the entry claims?** Partially. The estimator is honest (never clamps OOS ΔR², prints the negative, prints q on every row, names the replication rule in the config before the keyed run). The control is *deliberately harsh* in the way stated — but harsh in a way that biases against the claim, which the entry is right to flag and wrong to let be the only read: a peer basket that is nearly collinear with the taste basket means the OOS comparison is a fight between two noisy predictors of the same thing, and at n=36 holdout weeks and ~8–17 names per sector, that fight is underpowered. **What is the product worth if the answer comes back negative?** This is the load-bearing question.
