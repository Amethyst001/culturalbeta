**Abstract-thinking pass (before scoring)**

*What is measured.* A signed, out-of-sample (36-of-106 weeks) ΔR²: how much R² a sub-sector taste-basket regressor adds over SPY alone in a two-regressor OLS. Three orthogonal instruments claim to establish this: an OOS split (estimator), a 400-draw circular-rotation placebo (control against the factor being a rotation of itself), and a pre-registered disjoint window plus a price-only peer-basket ablation (control against regime drift and against the sector average carrying the channel). The desk also claims the *book-level* version: average add, share of flagged names (BH q ≤ 0.10, |t| ≥ 2), and cross-window replication.

*What would falsify it.* The three named outcomes on the Read page: (a) no flag survives BH across the 98 priced names; (b) the ablation cell — peer-only vs peer+taste — has both OOS intervals crossing zero on the majority of names; (c) the second window does not replicate (|ΔR²| difference larger than a regime-tolerable band). Any one is a negative.

*Does the estimator establish the claim?* Mostly yes, with two soft spots. The 70/36 split with 36 OOS weeks is statistically thin: a single week with an extreme return can move the per-name ΔR² by more than its own 90% CI. The desk addresses this by bounding the claim to book level (average add, share of names, replication) — good — but the per-name CSV columns (`oosDeltaR2FixedLo/Hi`, `oosDeltaR2RefitLo/Hi`) still invite a reader to misread a single name's interval. The fixed-coefficient interval will always be *narrower* than the refit interval on a real series; the page's rule that the *wider* one carries the claim is the right call, but the per-name bounds shipping as four CSV columns is the one place a downstream consumer can quietly use the wrong one. A single "use the wider" field or a documented CSV header note would close that.

The rotation placebo preserves cross-name alignment (the null keeps the book's correlation structure), which is the honest version of the control. It cannot rule out a factor that is *differently* correlated across names but still rotation-invariant in aggregate — a small residual, acceptable.

The ablation is the strongest control: a price-only, Qloo-free peer basket means that a null cell in peer+taste does not mean "taste is unpriced" — it means the sector average already carries the channel. The page says this. Good.

*Value under a negative.* The brief is explicit: a negative run yields a pre-registered falsification memo, the buyer is a factor researcher or buy-side quant testing a culture-channel hypothesis, and the cost is a Qloo subscription plus compute. There is no mass-market buyer. This is the honest floor and it is the right floor. A clean negative with this apparatus is a genuine research artifact worth something; a "no signal, wide CIs" result is worth less than the desk's own copy claims, because the apparatus's value depends on the signal surviving. The branch card's framing ("no mass-market buyer") is correct.

*The scope refusal.* One factor, no advice, no trading, no portfolio. This is the right scope for a prize-day entry. The desk is a measurement instrument, not a trading system, and saying so on the page is a strength. I will not treat the refusal as an oversight.

**Critical review — substance and execution**

The strongest thing in this entry is the honest-negative machinery. Most hackathon entries that claim to measure something build a demo that shows the positive case and a "what if it doesn't work?" card that is decorative. Here the negative branch is *priced* — it names the buyer, the cost, the artifact, and says plainly there is no mass-market version. That is a research-grade epistemic stance wrapped in a SPA, and it is what separates this from 80-range work.

The falsification section on the Read page (three named outcomes, live verdicts generated from the numbers by the code, not typed) is the right instrument: it turns the desk into a pre-registered test rather than a narrative. The regime label on the second window and the "not an identical-conditions check" sentence are the kind of detail a quant desk would expect from a factor paper.

The peer basket being Qloo-free and stated as such is the fix that a previous reviewer's confusion exposed, and it is done correctly. The consequence — "two regressors are correlated by construction, so a null cell means the sector average carries the channel" — is printed at the number. That is the honest way to read the ablation.

The stub harness running through the real pipeline with explicit "layout only, magnitudes not a Qloo result" watermarks on every screen is the correct discipline. No fabricated data in the repo, Qloo's terms respected, fixtures gitignored. The 33 unit tests, 260/260 CDP audit, zero-dependency Node 24 — the execution is clean.

*Weaknesses.*

1. **The 36-week OOS split is the estimator's soft underbelly.** The desk handles it by bounding the claim to book level, which is correct, but a reviewer who only reads the per-name CSV will see `oosDeltaR2FixedLo/Hi` and assume per-name precision. The wider-interval rule is printed at the number on the holding page, but the CSV export has four columns and no inline "use the wider" note. This is a documentation gap, not a math gap, but it will cost points with a non-quant reviewer.

2. **The taste factor is a single basket per sub-sector.** 101 names across 11 sub-sectors, each priced against its own sector basket. The "so one factor is comparable across the book" line on the Read page is the load-bearing claim, but a reviewer will ask: what happens to a name that sits on the border of two sub-sectors? The desk has no cross-sector reading. This is a scope choice, not a bug, but it limits the book-level average to "the book of 101 names" rather than "the consumer sector." The pre-registered second window does not diversify the factor; it just re-runs it in an earlier regime.

3. **The featured seven names (ELF, NKE, LULU, CMG, SBUX, MCD, DIS) carry an audience-level basket in addition to the sector reading.** This is a nice enrichment, but the screenshots show the audience basket as "structure only" with a stub note — the keyed run will populate it, but the design does not say whether the audience-level basket is *testable* the same way (OOS ΔR², placebo, ablation) or whether it is read in-sample only. If it is read in-sample only, the branch card's earlier correction ("that arm is only read in-sample") already flags it, but the holding page screenshot does not print that flag next to the audience-basket reading. A one-line note would close it.

4. **The rotation placebo's 400 draws are seeded from the config.** Good for reproducibility. The "same circular offset for every name" preserves cross-name correlation, which is the honest version. But 400 draws on a 106-week weekly series is a coarse grid; the placebo p-value (0.070 on the stub) is informative but will shift on real data. The desk should state the draw count and seed in the evidence footer so a reviewer can reproduce.

5. **The "RECOMPUTE FROM QLOO" action on the hosted demo is a single button under a timeout.** If the Qloo harness is slow or rate-limited, the demo will stall. The 90-second recording is the mitigation, but a reviewer watching the live URL will want to see the recompute succeed, not just the cache. The provision list should name the timeout behavior and what the page shows on failure.

*Execution quality.* The design is restrained and correct: serif display, muted palette, no dark mode, no animations, SVG hand-drawn. The taste map's four-corner labelling ("high market beta · against taste", etc.) is the right vocabulary for a factor desk. The holding page's side-by-side intervals with the wider-interval rule printed at the number is the most important screen and it is done well. The evidence desk's table — holding, sector, Qloo entity, workflow, basket types, cached response — is the kind of provenance table a research desk would print, and it is the screen that will win a quant reviewer's trust. The "stub harness" watermark on every screen is the discipline this entry needs and most competitors will not have.

**Abstract pass — before I look at the screens again**

*What is measured.* A signed out-of-sample ΔR² per name (R² from `return ~ SPY + taste` minus R² from `return ~ SPY`, both scored on the last 36 of 106 weeks, never clamped), plus the book-level digest: average ΔR², share of 98 names surviving BH q ≤ 0.10 with |t| ≥ 2, and cross-window replication. The estimand is narrow: *additive explanatory information over SPY, out of sample.* What the product *says* is much wider: "a consumer stock's price risk has a component from its customers' changing taste, and Qloo is the only clean instrument for it."

*The gap between the two.* The three named falsifiers (no flag survives; ablation null; no replication) can all print as **negative** while the wide claim remains entirely intact. That is because of two things the previous round handled at page level but which I cannot find resolved at the *abstract* level:

1. **The ablation has two null hypotheses and the desk collapses them.** The peer basket is price-only, so it is a sector-mean of the very regressions it tests — correlated with taste by construction, and a null also means the sector already carries the channel. But there is a second null: *Qloo is the only clean instrument.* If a price-only peer basket beats the taste basket across regimes, the Qloo data added nothing to a factor anyone can build from committed prices — and that falsifier is not among the three on the Read page, and the evidence screen does not print the Qloo call count against the peer arm. The consequence printed at the ablation band ("null means the sector average carries the channel, not that taste is unpriced") describes only the first null. The wide claim on the Read page therefore lacks its own falsifier. The entry should print, per arm, which null it kills: "peer-only beats taste ⇒ Qloo not the only clean instrument." One line, in config, at the number.
2. **The rotation placebo tests the wrong hypothesis for the wide claim.** Circular rotation of the trend *series* (and the brief actually says "of the whole book together" — a coarser version) controls for a factor that is a rotation of itself. A taste channel that is *genuinely* unpriced produces a *random-walk* velocity series, not a rotated one; the null distribution for "channel = noise" is not generated. On a 36-week OOS slice a real channel is likely to sit near the rotation null, so a p between ~0.05 and ~0.25 is uninterpretable between "rotation of itself" and "unpriced noise." The entry claims a placebo p at the number (ELF page: 0.090, bold) without naming which hypothesis that p addresses. The two null distributions — rotation vs random-walk — should both be printed, and the decision rule should say which one falsifies.

What would falsify the entry's own claim, then, is a *fourth* outcome: **the peer-only arm outperforms taste in both windows** (and Qloo's marginal call count adds nothing to a costless price-only factor). Add it.

*What the product is worth if negative.* This is where I split from Abhibots, and it is the crux of my scoring. Abhibots prices the floor as "a pre-registered falsification memo, bought by a factor researcher or buy-side quant, at the cost of a Qloo subscription plus compute." I don't accept that as the floor, for two reasons:

- **A negative ablation at 48% (the stub) is not a negative for Qloo.** 52% of names still earn the taste basket over peers — that is a segmentation map: the taste channel is live in a subset of the consumer sector, dead in the rest, and the map tells you *where*. That is an actionable artifact with a positive reading. The entry's stub copy doesn't say this; it only prices the all-negative branch. The value of a *mixed* result — the most likely outcome — is unpriced in the entry.
- **The buyer named is not the buyer.** "A factor researcher testing a culture-channel hypothesis" would not pay for a consumer-only desk. The buyer for a negative or mixed memo is the one already in the room: the Qloo team. The desk's evidence provenance (which workflow, which entity, which cached response, per holding) is exactly what Qloo needs to sell the cultural graph to finance as a distribution channel. A *negative* culture channel in equities is a marketing asset for Qloo's data business, not a research product. The entry's honest floor is therefore: (a) the mixed-outcome segmentation map, (b) the pre-registered apparatus as a public benchmark of "how to test a cultural graph on prices," and (c) the entry as evidence of Qloo's financial-grade integration value. None of that requires a positive ΔR².

*The scope refusal.* One factor, no advice, no trading, no portfolio. That is the correct scope for this entry's claims — and it is also what the previous round's "product angle" suggestions (quarterly re-runs, alert subscriptions, LOIs) quietly broke. I disagree with Abhibots' highest-leverage change on that ground, below.

*The regime problem, which neither prior round named.* The 2022–2024 window is the pandemic-era taste regime: recommendations from a lockdown consumer are a *different* cultural graph than 2025–26. If a miss on window 2 is regime-caused, the replication check is weak by construction. The entry labels it "different regime" (good) but never states that a *replication* across regimes is not a control for taste-channel stability — it is a control for *regime drift of the factor*. The placebo p, the ablation, and the second window all carry the same implicit "which regime does this number describe?" question. The verdict sentence must carry it.
