## Where I land, and why I'm docking where Abhibots didn't

**I agree with Abhibots on the bones**: 19 TI, 21 Design, 17 Impact, 22 Idea, 79 total. The rigor (uncapped OOS ΔR², autocorrelation-preserving circular-rotation placebo, provenance to the request, gitignored caches, 326 passing CDP checks) is genuinely unusual for a hackathon, and the honesty of the null-state design is the right call. **Where I sharpen**: Abhibots graded the statistics almost as if this were a journal paper. TI here is not "did you run Breusch–Pagan" — the rubric literally reads *"How thoroughly and skillfully does the project use Qloo?"* Through that lens, the entry's Qloo depth is thinner than it looks: two endpoints (`/v2/trending`, `/v2/insights`), velocity fold-change, leave-one-out affinity. The entire flavor of Qloo's 250M-entity taste graph — music, film, dining, fashion, crossovers between them — shows up as **affinity weights to six sibling consumer brands**. The most striking data point in the holding screenshot is that ELF's "taste basket" is *mostly NKE, LULU, CMG, SBUX, MCD, DIS* — i.e., the basket of e.l.f. Beauty's customers is other consumer equities, which is almost tautological for a seven-consumer-brand book. That is not a layout defect; it is a concept-to-data mismatch that the real Qloo run will expose or entrench. Abhibots missed this entirely — it is the biggest quality-of-idea risk in the entry.

**Where I disagree outright**: Abhibots' highest-leverage change is a bundle of committed public fixtures + a reproduce script + rendered static report + OLS diagnostics, "+3 to +5 total". Three problems:

1. It's partly **disallowed by the entry's own constraints**. `fixtures/qloo/` is gitignored *because Qloo's terms forbid republishing their data*. 2. It **underweights the single most powerful artifact available**: a live demo that judges can re-run *with their own eyes*. The forum shows other teams offering judges capped access codes — that's the norm here. A hosted URL with a precomputed, server-cached keyed panel (computed once when the key lands, cached, served from cache so judges never trigger rate limits) plus a visible "RECOMPUTE FROM QLOO" working end-to-end is worth more than a static report, and it costs a night.
3. "Add ~2 more OLS diagnostics" as a top recommendation is misread scale: with n=106 and one factor, 7 holdings, HW or Breusch–Pagan moves no one's opinion. The real statistical vulnerability is the **multiple-testing disclosure**, which the evidence card already states — good.

Also: Abhibots' "+2 TI for diagnostics, +1–2 for a static report" is a conservative read of a hackathon where the TI criterion is Qloo-usage. A live, demonstrable, key-hydrated end-to-end run pushes TI toward 22–23 by itself.
