## Abstract thinking: what is actually being measured

Strip away the screens and the entry makes **three claims**, nested:

The instrument is a sector taste basket: ln(fold-change in trending velocity) of affinity-weighted non-universe Qloo recommendations, with re-weighting (never zero-fill) on missing weeks.

**Claim B (incremental value).** That component adds predictive power *beyond* what the sector already shows. This is the ablation: equal-weight peer basket fit first, taste added second, judged **out of sample** on a 70/36 split, printed signed. The stub run already prints −0.31 pts on 52% of names — and the screenshots show the evidence page rendering the harder, negative case, with the second window failing to replicate (basket mean OOS ΔR² +0.14, placebo p 0.531, "NOT SEPARABLE").

**Claim C (the thesis).** "Qloo is the *only* clean instrument." This is the one claim that is *false by construction* in this entry: the ablation's own peer basket and any public trend index are alternatives the desk now prices on. The entry's honest move is to have quietly migrated from "only" to "how much does it add over the obvious alternative." I'll hold them to that migration.

**What would falsify it, concretely:** (i) OOS taste gain over peers ≤ 0 on most names (already the stub case); (ii) placebo p > 0.10, i.e., the cross-name alignment is indistinguishable from rotation; (iii) non-replication on the pre-registered disjoint window; (iv) zero or near-zero BH-corrected flags across the book. All four are *printable by the desk*, which is exactly the right design. What would falsify the *idea* is a positive result surviving all four on real data — a provision cannot buy that, so the whole question of "worth if negative" is the one that decides this entry's tier.

**Are the instrument, estimator and control actually establishing what's claimed?**

- **Instrument — sound with one soft spot.** Self-exclusion by entity id *and* normalised name is correct. But "every name in the universe is excluded from every basket" is overclaimed: a name's *subsidiaries, franchises, and co-branded titles* sit in its own sector basket. This is the residual self-contamination channel, and a one-page "exclusion integrity" appendix (entity ids, excluded-neighbour counts, name-normalisation rules) would convert it from a vulnerability into documented care.
- **Estimator — mostly sound, with one real flaw.** The **400 circular rotations are a weak placebo.** Rotating *all* baskets by the same amount is a coarse scramble that deliberately destroys the cross-name alignment the entry itself calls its strength. A stronger placebo — randomising basket *identity* (each name re-scores against a random sector's basket), ~500 draws — is cheap, uses data already in hand, and would tighten the p-values that decide the rotation verdict. The 100-week sample floor, Student-t p, BH across the priced book, and the never-clamped OOS re-fit are all correct, and the "allow it to be negative" discipline is genuinely rare.

## The screens are lying by omission (and by stub), and the team knows it

This is the most important execution finding and neither reviewer surfaced it properly. The screenshots are a **stub harness** run, and the stub fabricates the culture signal in a way that is *biased toward a positive result*:

- The stub's rotation baseline (`+0.94`) is a coincidence of the fabricated taste, not a measured property of real data.

So the entire evidentiary weight of the entry currently rests on **code that does not run on real data**. That is not a provision — the keyed run is the single artifact that converts this from "a very serious-looking layout" to "a measurement." I agree with Claude that I score it *as it will exist on prize day with every provision in place*, but I'm much harder than both earlier reviewers on what the screens themselves prove: **they prove the design, the honesty, and the pipeline — and nothing about the answer.**

One more concrete execution crack that no one flagged: the evidence table shows ELF, ULTA, EL, COTY, IPAR all cached to the same file `basket-sector-beauty.json`.
