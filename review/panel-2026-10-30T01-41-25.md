## Real critical review

### Substance

The entry's core claim is narrow and correct: a consumer stock's price risk has a component that comes from its customers' changing taste, Qloo is the only clean instrument for that component, and the desk measures how much that instrument adds over the obvious alternative. The falsifier structure — four named results that would kill the claim, generated from the numbers by code — is the best part of the round. The pre-registered second window fixed in config before the key is the right discipline. The price-only ablation arm isolating Qloo cost is the cleanest experimental design in the entry.

The weak point is that "cultural beta" is a label ahead of the evidence. The construct is not distinguished from plain consumer sentiment: trending velocity of a sub-sector's recommended brands and films could be a lagging consumer-confidence proxy, not a taste channel. The entry should print a named exclusion against a published sentiment index. Without that sentence, a sharp reader will say: you have not shown this is cultural rather than cyclical.

The ablation's circularity is the second weak point, stated more precisely: if the sector price-only peer basket is constructed from the same 101 names the taste basket is constructed to represent, the ablation is testing "can Qloo add anything on top of what the sector mean already knows?" The negative stub value (−0.31) is consistent with that, and the page says so. But the page does not say the stronger thing: that a negative additive cell is the *expected* answer if the sector mean already carries the taste, and that the entry's real finding in that case is the size of the sector mean's absorption, not a refutation of taste as a construct. That sentence is missing.

The power structure is honest but consequential. 98 names with BH at 5% expect at most five flags. 36 OOS weeks gives every per-name p an interval wide enough to swallow a sign change. The entry says this. The consequence is that the BH flag is a screening device — "this name deserves a human look" — not a verdict. The entry says this too. What it does not say, and should: the flag's positive predictive value on a negative ablation is near zero, because the ablation cell tells you the instrument is not additive in the sector, and a per-name flag within a non-additive sector is noise. That is a one-sentence addition that would close the last gap in the Read page's honesty.
