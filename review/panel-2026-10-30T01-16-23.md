## reviewer-2 · product
**Model:** Abhibots · Claude Fable 5.1 (abhibots/claude-fable-5-1) · saw the screenshots
**Charter:** does this read as a desk a consumer-equity PM would actually use; what is missing or bloated
**Latency:** 33306 ms

Before the numbers, the abstract argument, since that is where the earlier reviewers were too soft.

**What is actually being measured.** Two estimands: (a) per name, ΔR² = R²(returns ~ SPY + taste basket) − R²(returns ~ SPY), out-of-sample on 36 held-out weeks, fit on 70; and (b) the ablation estimand, the signed difference between the taste basket and a same-sector equal-weight peer basket as a second regressor. Both are conditional, not causal: with 36 OOS weeks and 106 in-sample, the OOS ΔR² estimator is coarse at the individual name level, and what the desk can really resolve at name level is the *sign and direction* of the gain, not its magnitude. The honest estimand is the book-level statement — "fraction of names where taste adds, and by how much on average" — plus the replication verdict on the pre-registered disjoint window. That framing matters: a sharp-judged entry should print "we are not claiming point-estimate precision at name level; the claim is at book level, with replication" and that framing is, to my eye, the single most under-weighted quality issue in this round, more so than any of the three named findings.

**What falsifies it.** Three separate falsifications, each visible on a screen: zero flags after BH across the whole book while the book verdict reads positive; the ablation cell (taste over peers) reading negative with both intervals straddling zero on the real run, meaning the peer basket already carries the taste channel and Qloo's marginal instrument value is zero; and the replication window's mean ΔR² ≤ 0.5 pts. The entry's claim is narrow enough that all three are printed.

**Do the instrument, estimator, and control establish what is claimed?** Instrument: yes, defensibly — ln(fold-change in trending velocity) with a documented fallback, re-weighted missing weeks, schema-enforced calls, pre-registered floor, split, seed and flag rule in config before the key. That is genuinely strong. Estimator: the two-bootstrap fix is the right one and closes the last round's biggest finding correctly — the refit draw carries nothing from its training resample into an independent test resample, so the fixed-coefficient interval is known to understate uncertainty, and printing both with the wider one carrying the claim is the honest resolution. Control: the ablation against a same-sector peer basket is the correct *design* of control, but I want to disagree with the two prior reviewers here and name their shared weakness: **the sector peer basket is not a true control for "taste channel" because it is itself built from the same Qloo recommendations, re-weighted by affinity within the sector rather than by entity identity**. A null ablation does not prove the taste channel is absent — it proves the peer basket is at least as good. A reviewer who is actually skeptical will note that the two regressors are correlated, which inflates the taste coefficient's variance even if the point estimate is positive. The entry should preempt this with one sentence on the Evidence desk: "the peer basket is a same-instrument control, not a no-instrument control; a null ablation falsifies 'Qloo's marginal value over a sector average', not 'taste is unpriced'." That one sentence is the only place I see the abstract claim and the control misaligned, and it is fixable on the page, not in the estimator.

**What the product is worth if the answer is negative.** I take issue with Abhibots's +6 to Potential Impact on the theory of a "named, paying consumer": a buy-side quant who is testing a pre-registered culture-channel factor at 36 OOS weeks and 98 priced names is a small buyer, and the price (Qloo subscription plus compute) is not the desk's price — the desk is a method artifact and a verdict, not a data product, because the data cannot be republished. The honest valuation of the negative case is: it is a publication-grade falsification with a pre-registered replication, worth to an academic researcher or a factor-shop memo, roughly the price of a well-cited working paper. That is a real but narrow wedge. The entry should say that wedge on the Read page in one line: "if this run lands negative, the artifact is a pre-registered falsification memo, not a signal; the memo is the product."
