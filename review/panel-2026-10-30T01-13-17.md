## Abstract-level review (before any score)

**What is actually being measured.** The entry's real output is not "cultural beta" — that's the headline. The measured quantity is a *conditional increment*: ΔR², out-of-sample, of adding the Qloo taste basket to a model that already contains SPY and the same-sector peer basket, read against three controls (pre-registered disjoint window, circular-rotation placebo, and the refit bootstrap). That framing is the intellectually honest one, and the branch card now states it correctly: the peer basket is the baseline, and Qloo must earn its place on top.

**What would falsify it.** The pre-registered null: the ablation cell (taste over peers) has a refit interval containing zero, and that holds on the second window. The desk is built to *print* that outcome, which is the right design.

**Where the apparatus still overreaches itself.** One sentence in the "if no" branch is a claim, not a design: "the sector-peer model is *itself* the usable product (…from committed prices, no vendor) … so 'no signal' stays inspectable." That sentence is false on its own terms. The peer basket's **in-sample** R² and its OOS ablation arm are computed on the same 106 fitted weeks, so that arm is in-sample, not out-of-sample. An OOS-increment read on the peer arm is only available when the OOS model drops the peers; the OOS ablation cell measures taste *additively over* peers. So if the control says no, the desk's real fallback value is: *a pre-committed, reproducible consumer-book price panel plus the negative-result evidence itself* — which is worth something, but less than that sentence admits. A quant reader will catch this, and a quant reader is exactly who matters.

**What this round's fixes did and didn't do.** The refit bootstrap is the right call and it closes the estimator caveat; the in-sample cell leaving the band closes the "free positive evidence" layout defect; the stub collapse closes the data-bug mimic. But three things survive, and none of them are cosmetic:

1. **The 200-draw refit interval is the load-bearing one, and it's the one that's weakest.** It's *shorter* than the 400-draw fixed-coefficient interval it now has to carry the harder claim. "Where the two disagree, the wider one carries the claim" is a good rule, but the rule only works if the reader knows which is which, and the rule text appears only in the method footer, not on the ablation band or the holding page.
2. **The two windows are not windows.** "106 aligned weekly returns (2024-10-14 → 2026-10-26)" is ~2.04 years = ~106 weeks; "the 106 weeks immediately before" would run to roughly 2022-10. The replication requirement (mean ΔR² > 0 on the disjoint window) is a statement about the **pre-2024 regime** — which, for a *taste* factor, is the most plausible positive regime, because 2022-24 trend churn was high. So the replication test is easier in the direction the desk hopes for, and the desk currently presents it as an identical-conditions check.
3. **The placebo is aligned.** 400 circular rotations of the *same* book with cross-name alignment preserved means the null distribution has the same correlation structure as the data. p=0.070 against an aligned rotation can be a flatter statement than it reads.

**4. Product angle**

The product is the **consumer-book audit feed**, not the signal: the `GET /api/v1/panel`, `GET /api/v1/alerts`, `GET /export/panel.csv` surface, where a flag fires only where it survives BH, its own OOS test, and the book placebo. The buyer is a retail-platform or consumer-equity data desk that holds a book and wants a pre-registered, two-interval, replication-surviving exposure read. The feed is worth paying for when three things are true: (1) it updates weekly and the **alert contract is the product** — "flag fires" is the unit of value, not the panel; (2) the BH q and placebo p are **in the API payload**, not just the UI — otherwise the API is a CSV with extra steps; (3) the **negative-result path is a priced tier** — a desk that wants the panel and the ablation verdict with or without a positive flag is a real customer, and the "if the control says no" branch is that tier's sales deck. If none of those are true, it's an artifact, not a business.
