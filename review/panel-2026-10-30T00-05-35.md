## 4. The product angle

There is a product, and it's the alerts feed — but today it's "available," not "worth paying for." To make it pay:

- **The buyer** is a consumer-sector analyst, a small fund, or a PE operating partner running a consumer platform (beauty, restaurant, retail roll-ups) — a *research* buyer, not an HFT desk, since the desk explicitly does not trade or advise.
- **What makes it worth paying for:** (a) a **weekly subscription digest** scoped to the buyer's *held* consumer positions, not the full universe — "taste headwinds across your book"; (b) a **stable, documented, versioned API** (`/api/v1/alerts` already exists) with an SLA, so a portfolio tracker can pull it; (c) the **triple robustness as a trust feature** — OOS + placebo + second window is exactly what a skeptical buyer of a "cultural signal" needs to justify a subscription; (d) **11 named sub-sector packs** as the SKU structure, which turns 101 names into 11 sellable feeds with different buyers.
- The concrete multiplication: the feed is the product, the 11 sub-sector packs are the SKU, the triple robustness + provenance Evidence desk is the trust moat that justifies the price. None of this breaks the no-advice/no-trading constraint — it's selling measurement and inspection, which is the boundary the team has already drawn.

## 8. Verdict

**Credible mid-pack-to-top-3, not a clear front-runner.** On substance this is one of the strongest *engineering* entries in a Qloo hackathon, and the jump from 85.3 to 87 is earned by the state-tag fix, the plain-English page, and the multiplicity/replication rigor. Two things keep "competitive for a top prize" from a safe call: (1) the headline "only clean instrument" claim is asserted, not ablated — land #3 and it moves honestly to top-3; (2) the value is probabilistically thin, and a keyed run that lands weak, single- or zero-flag, non-replicating will read as a null study dressed as a desk. The team's honesty (designed cold state, inspectable "no alert," "does not replicate" branch) is good science and good craft, but a prize panel rewards a *demonstrated* result, not a demonstrated willingness to report one. Mid-pack credible; top-3 if the ablation lands **and** the data cooperates — I will not call it a safe top-prize winner on the data risk alone.
