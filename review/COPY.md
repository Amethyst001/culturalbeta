# Copy review — Cultural Beta desk

Language review of every user-visible string in `desk/app.js`, `desk/index.html` and `desk/interactions.js`.
Read-only: nothing in `desk/` was edited. This file is the only artefact.

**Verdict key.** KEEP = already the best sentence for its job. TIGHTEN = same meaning, fewer words or cleaner syntax. REWRITE = unclear, wrong voice, or ambiguous against the code beside it. Every proposal preserves the fact, number, hedge and caveat of the original; nothing is softened and no claim is added.

**Voice assumed.** Laconic analyst. States the number, names the limit once, moves on. Not a marketing page (no adjectives doing the work of a number), not a chatbot (no "great question", no exclamation), not a compliance memo (no caveat that repeats the caveat above it). The desk is allowed one dry judgment per screen ("which is little", "not a kill"); it is not allowed to congratulate itself for honesty.

**Not reviewed:** JSON keys, identifiers, log text, code comments, server-supplied strings that the page renders verbatim (agent answers, `att.note`, `EVIDENCE.factorConstruction`, budget line labels and notes), and the 101 tickers/company names.

## 10. Inconsistencies

Same concept, different words
1. **cultural beta / taste beta / taste factor / cultural factor.** `taste beta` ×2 (#58, #60); `taste factor` ×7 — twice in the cold banner (#56), in the tail of the pooled sentence (#156 and its cold twin #273), across the sentiment copy (#267, #271) and in the replication line (#272); `cultural factor` ×1; `cultural beta` everywhere else. The quantity has one name. Use *cultural beta* for the number, *cultural factor* for the thing, and delete `taste beta` / `taste factor`.
2. **holding / name / stock / ticker / brand.** The row is a `Holding` in two column headers (and four mobile labels), a `name` in most sentences, a `stock` in the read page, a `ticker` in the replay table, and a `brand` only when it means a Qloo entity. Recommend: *name* for a row, *ticker* for the symbol, *brand/entity* only for the Qloo object. "Holding" survives only in `#/holding/` and the page title, if at all.
3. **priced, three senses.** (a) "has a price history" — `market betas, live`, `MARKET.universe.priced`; (b) "has a cultural beta" — `80 of 101 priced`, `names priced`, `of 80 priced`; (c) "measured against" — `each priced against its own sub-sector's taste basket` (limits card, once). Sense (b) is used 40+ times, (a) twice, (c) once, and the cold strip prints (a) and (b) on one screen. Fix: say *priced* only for (b) and *with a complete price history* for (a); (c) reads acceptably in place but is a third sense, not the same one.
4. **flag / taste risk / headwind / watchlist / alert / feed arms / fires.** Seven labels for one mechanism. The column says `taste risk`, the strip says `taste-risk flags`, the prose says `headwind`, the API says `watchlist`, and the branch card says `arms` then `firing`. Recommend: *flag* (the mark), *headwind* (what it means), *alert* (what the feed emits) — and retire "arms the feed".
5. **re-derive / recompute / re-run / re-estimate.** Button `Re-derive`, error `Recompute refused`, tool `re-run`, replay `re-estimate`, chip `Recompute … live`. Four verbs for three actions; the button's verb should win.
6. **"second" is overloaded.** `second window` (replication), `second reading` (#101, audience basket), `second construction` (permutation null). Reserve *second window* for replication and rename the audience block.
7. **panel / book / desk.** Mostly a good three-way split (panel = computed artefact, book = the 101 names, desk = the product), but it leaks: "printed on the desk" means "on this page" (#130), "the desk server", "the desk quit". Recommend the split be stated once and held.

Same word, different meanings
8. **gate** — three meanings: the live Qloo switch (`the live Qloo gate is off`), the reproduction check (`Rebuild gate`, #175), the attestation (`gate pass`, `attestation-gate`). Reserve *gate* for the attestation; call the rebuild one a *check*.
9. **flagged** — a taste-risk call (42×) and "kept in the roster with a marker" (#78). The second is the one to change.
10. **live** — live Qloo (#59 context), live prices (`market betas, live`, ledger `Live`), live re-estimate (`Re-estimated at inference`). Reserve *live* for a Qloo request made now.
11. **signal** — the Signal column, a signal strength, Qloo entities that "signal" a basket (#131), and `signals` in the receipt. Only the column and "signal strength" are the same idea.
12. **screen** — verb (`Filter`/`screen`), noun (`A screen, not a signal service`), adjective (`the same-sector basket` is not one but `screenPassesBeforeBH` is), and `Clears the screen` as a table header. Disambiguate the header at least.
13. **reading** — the finding, the act of loading (`Reading the panel`), "a reading with its error" (an estimate), and "second reading" (a second basket). Four senses in three pages.

Capitalisation and title case
14. Headings are sentence case and consistent (`Taste is a risk factor.`, `The book, priced two ways`, `When Qloo's numbers stop moving`). Keep it. The drift is in links and h1s: `back to the desk` is the only lowercase link label (#77); tab `Taste map` vs h1 `Taste map.` vs tab `Evidence` vs h1 `Evidence desk.`; `Desk` tab covers both the desk and the read page.
15. Column headers are title case (`Market beta`, `Signal`), chips and tags are lowercase (`post-hoc · not a test of the claim`), states are capitalised (`Attestation: pass.`). This is a coherent system; the exceptions are `Rebuild gate`, `Live`/`Withheld` and `TICKER`-style table headers in the replay view (`Ticker`).

Numbers and punctuation
16. **Double sign**: `added +0.87` (#26) against `adds 0.87` on the landing line (#17). Strip `+` after a verb; keep it when the number stands alone.
17. **r² / R²**: 8 vs 51. Pick `R²` everywhere (it is a named statistic, not a unit). #17 and #26 are kept for their wording; the symbol in them is normalised by this rule.
18. **`<.001` vs `< 0.001`**: one cell vs the rest (#85).
19. **`80/101` vs `80 of 101`**: the state pill is the only slash form, on three routes (#15, #82, #106).
20. **`pts` vs `points`**: cells say `pts`, prose says `points`, and the read page uses both within one section. Rule: `pts` in a cell, `points` in a sentence.
21. **Ranges**: `19–31` (unspaced en dash) vs `IQR 0.4 – 1.2` (spaced). Negatives come from `toFixed()` as ASCII `-` (`-0.21`), so a hyphen is a sign and an en dash is a span. One range style: unspaced en dash.
22. **`80/101` health check**: two paragraphs quote the chance-flag count differently — `A hundred names … about five` (#36) vs `80 … about 4` (#146). Same computation, two answers.
23. **Dates**: `2025-09-28` (data week), `2025-12-01` / `2025-12-22` (replay), `22:14Z` (hhmm), `2026-10-31T00:34:12` (receipt revision, no `Z`). Rule: data weeks as ISO days; instants as `YYYY-MM-DD HH:MMZ`; if a full ISO datetime is printed, end it with `Z` or say "local".
24. **Tense** is consistent (present habitual for the desk, present perfect for Qloo's revisions, past for a replay). No drift found.

## 12. Verdict

This is the most disciplined copy in the repository and among the most disciplined I have reviewed for a product of this age: 177 of 273 strings need nothing, the long paragraphs are long because they carry a caveat that cannot be shortened without softening it, and the refusal to oversell ("No Qloo. No factor.", "Measured, and not charged.", "a book to read, not a screen or a ranking of investability") is a voice, not a policy. Two things hold it back from a 9. First, terminology drift at scale: `priced`, `holding`, `gate`, `taste beta` and `live` each carry two or three meanings, and the drift is worst exactly where a first-time judge looks (the cold state, the first table, the replay toggle). Second, a recurring tic of narrating its own candour — "the desk says so", "the desk prints both", "the desk states it as one" — which is the one place the laconic analyst starts talking about himself instead of the number. Fix the headline derivation, the cold-state vocabulary, the replay dates and the `priced`/`holding` pair and this reads as a desk written by one careful person, which is the highest compliment this genre allows.

**Language score: 8 / 10.**
